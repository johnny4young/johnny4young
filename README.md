## About

**Backend engineer** specialized in **Node.js**, **TypeScript**, and **Go**, with deep focus on **distributed systems**, **developer tooling**, and **production-grade LLM applications** — plus a growing line of **native Apple apps in Swift** (macOS · iOS · iPadOS).

I design APIs and platforms that survive real traffic — clean boundaries, strong observability, and the kind of architecture that lets teams ship fast without breaking things. Lately, much of my work sits where **backend engineering meets AI**: agent workflows, MCP servers, retrieval pipelines, and on-device models wired into **local-first products** that keep your data on your machine.

> Pragmatism over hype. Fewer abstractions over clever ones. Tools that get out of the developer's way.

### 🤖 [Janusly](https://github.com/johnny4young/janusly)
**AI operator for business workflows**

Design, run, recover, and operate workflows from **one Go executable**: an embedded React control plane, PostgreSQL 18 as the durable queue, and OpenTelemetry built in. AI is part of the loop, not glued on top — Claude-backed workflow generation, run explanations, and semantic failure recovery, each with a deterministic fallback and human approval before anything mutates. Speaks **MCP both ways**: workflows consume external MCP tools, and agents can inspect and operate Janusly through its own MCP server.

## 🧭 How I Build

- **Boring tech, sharp execution.** Pick proven tools. Spend the novelty budget where it actually matters.
- **Observability is not optional.** If you can't see it in production, you don't own it.
- **Small interfaces, composable parts.** Most complexity comes from premature abstractions, not missing ones.
- **Type the boundaries.** TypeScript, Go, and Swift aren't religions — they're contracts that catch bugs before users do.
- **AI is a system, not a prompt.** Real LLM apps live or die by retrieval, evals, guardrails, and observability — not the model name.
- **Ship, then sharpen.** A merged PR teaches more than a perfect plan.
